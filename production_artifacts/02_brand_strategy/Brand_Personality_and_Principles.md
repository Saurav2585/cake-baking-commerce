# Brand Personality and Principles

## Personality

- **Capable:** knows the difference between relevant formats, sizes and uses.
- **Encouraging:** helps a newer baker move forward without talking down to them.
- **Considered:** curates and explains instead of overwhelming.
- **Sensory:** remembers that baking is tactile, aromatic and celebratory.
- **Straightforward:** distinguishes facts, guidance, simulation and missing information.

## Governing principles

### 1. Precision should feel welcoming

Use exact weights, dimensions and application terms, then explain them in ordinary language. Never trade correctness for cuteness.

### 2. Help before hype

Lead with fit, format, use and next action. Avoid “best”, “premium quality”, “trusted”, “top-selling” and similar assertions without evidence.

### 3. Curate without gatekeeping

Serve first-time and experienced makers in the same system. Explain specialist terms once; do not imply that expertise or expensive materials determine belonging.

### 4. Make appetite useful

Sensory language may create anticipation, but must connect to a recipe, technique or product function. Do not turn mood into food, health or performance claims.

### 5. Be explicit about uncertainty

Use “Information not provided” for missing critical ingredient, allergen and storage fields (D-017). Do not infer suitability.

### 6. Celebrate the making, not an invented mythology

Focus on present-day acts—measuring, folding, filling, finishing, packing. Do not invent founder history, years of expertise, sourcing stories or credentials.

## Behaviour by moment

| Moment | Brand behaviour |
|---|---|
| Exploration | Inviting, broad, task-led |
| Comparison | Calm, exact, scannable |
| Uncertainty | Plain explanation and visible unknowns |
| Recipe planning | Encouraging, measured, user-controlled |
| Cart/checkout simulation | Direct, transparent and non-transactional |
| Error/empty state | Specific, helpful and never blaming |

## Anti-patterns

- Childish cupcake mascots, novelty puns as the primary voice or gender-coded “pink bakery” conventions.
- Excessive French/European luxury cues or inaccessible culinary jargon.
- Competitor-derived claims, taxonomy language used as a slogan, or marketplace seller framing.
- Claims of guaranteed results, food safety, dietary suitability, professional endorsement or product superiority.
