# Naming Exploration

## Method and constraints

Names were developed for an original curated retailer, not for its fictional product brands. Scores use 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) against nine criteria. They are strategic screening judgments, not legal or availability findings.

**Mandatory before production:** independent trademark clearance, Indian company/name checks, linguistic screening, domain and social-handle checks. No availability is claimed here.

## Shortlist

### 1. Whisklane — recommended

Suggests a clear route through baking without restricting the assortment to ingredients or decoration. Easy to say and type in Indian English; contemporary, friendly and capable. The “lane” idea supports navigation, guidance and a flexible visual system. Risk: “whisk” can over-index on tools; breadth must be established through descriptor and department cues.

### 2. Crumbloom

Distinctive and sensory, joining the made result with growth/creativity. Risk: softer and more decorative; could under-signal tools, packaging and practical precision.

### 3. Batterfolk

Warm, inclusive and community-minded without claiming a real community. Risk: “folk” can feel casual and batter excludes some product occasions.

### 4. Ovenora

Compact, ownable-sounding and visually flexible. Risk: invented spelling needs repetition; “oven” may imply appliances or finished baking rather than supplies.

### 5. Pantryrise

Connects stocked readiness with making progress. Risk: two-word compound is slightly less fluid, and “pantry” may broaden beyond baking.

## Recommendation

Advance **Whisklane** as the strategic recommendation, paired during exploration with a category descriptor such as “Baking ingredients & supplies”. The recommendation is not a final selection and must not enter production until the human gate and external validation are complete.

## Names not advanced

The remaining candidates either read as more generic, create spelling/pronunciation friction, narrow the assortment, or lean too far toward luxury, craft or decorative softness. Score proximity does not supersede the qualitative fit assessment.
